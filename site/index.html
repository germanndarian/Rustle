Rustle for macOS

Leaves are falling on your desktop.

Maple, oak and ginkgo leaves drift down over your screen, pile up along the bottom and on top of your windows, and crunch when you sweep the cursor through them. Now and then the wind blows them away.

This menu works.

It's the menu Rustle puts in your menu bar, and it runs the leaves on this page. Try Heavy, turn off Wind Blows Leaves Away and watch the pile grow, or pick up the rake.

With a tool in hand, hold the mouse button to rake or blow. Esc or a right-click puts it away. Leaves on Windows lets leaves settle on top of this menu, the way they settle on your windows.

What the leaves do

Fall
Each leaf swings and rocks on its way down like a real one, and some tumble end over end. Variable lets the leaf fall pick up and ease off over the day.
Pile up
They gather along the bottom of the screen and slump to a natural slope. They settle on any window with room above it, too.
Crunch
Sweep the cursor through the pile and the leaves fly up with a dry crunch. The sound is made live from thousands of tiny crackles, so it never repeats.
Blow away
Every so often a gust sweeps across and carries part of the pile off the screen, more often when the pile is deep. Turn it off and the leaves stay.
Crumble
Leaves that have lain in the pile a while dry out, turn a dull brown and crumble into flakes, so the pile thins slowly from below, the way snow melts. The deeper the pile, the sooner they go.
Rake
Pick up the rake, hold the mouse button and drag. Leaves catch in the tines and come along until you let go, so you can rake them into heaps.
Blow
The leaf blower lifts leaves off the pile and pushes them wherever it points, with a soft electric whoosh. Scroll to aim the nozzle.
Slide off
Drag a window and its leaves slide off with it. Close or minimize it and they drop. Switch Spaces and they come back with the window.
Stay out of the way
Clicks go straight through the leaves. Screenshots and screen sharing show your desktop without them, unless you turn on Show in Screenshots.
Rest
About 4% of one processor core and 55 MB of memory while leaves fall, and about 1% once they've settled. It pauses while your screen sleeps or locks.

Six kinds of leaf

Every leaf in Rustle is drawn in code, 36 of them in all, from golden ginkgo to wine-dark sweetgum. These are the ones that fall on your desktop.

Maples

Acer saccharum, A. rubrum, A. palmatumDrawings 1–13 · sugar, red and Japanese

Oaks

Quercus robur, Q. albaDrawings 14–18 · English and white oak

Birch and aspen

Ginkgo

Sweetgum

Let the leaves in.

Rustle is a small native app for macOS 14 or later. Open it once and it lives in your menu bar. It isn't notarized yet, so the first time, right-click it and choose Open.
